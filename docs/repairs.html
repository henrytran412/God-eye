Nine Repairs, Zero Gain

A detector trained at a Beijing intersection keeps 3% of its accuracy at a German one. Nine label-free attempts to win that back, what each was testing, and why none of them worked.

01

What broke

Detection accuracy does not degrade gracefully across intersections. It falls off a cliff — but only for one class.

3D AP, moderateDAIR-V2X-ITUMTrafRetained
Car @ IoU 0.569.701.833%
Pedestrian @ IoU 0.2548.8743.1188%
Cyclist @ IoU 0.2557.6343.1375%

Pedestrian and Cyclist survive the move almost intact. Car does not. The difference between them is not the object — it is the threshold: Car is scored at IoU 0.5, the other two at 0.25. That single fact shapes everything below.

Which numbers are which. Diagnosis figures in §2 come from all 2,160 target frames, where the Car baseline reads 0.79. Repair figures come from a disjoint 1,620-frame evaluation split, where it reads 1.83. Both are the INT8 model; the project's published 0.13 is the FP16 model on 2,160 frames. All three are correct and measure different things.

The harness reproduces the published in-domain 69.70 exactly, which is what licenses the rest.

02

The diagnosis that chose the repairs

Before fixing anything, we measured what a perfect fix would even be worth. Substitute ground truth for one predicted quantity, re-score, read off the ceiling.

Perfect heading is worth 0.06 AP. This overturned the project's own hypothesis, which held that orientation was the fragile component. Heading really does degrade — yaw spread widens from 12.4° to 22.3°, and vehicles within ±5° of true fall from 88% to 35% — it simply cannot move the score on its own.

The reason is in the last row. Fixing one axis buys almost nothing; fixing three buys 13.31. The failure is conjunctive. Matched cars carry four errors simultaneously:

Each error alone is survivable. Together they put the box on the wrong side of IoU 0.5 — and Pedestrian and Cyclist, judged at 0.25, stay on the right side of theirs. That is the whole class pattern, explained.

One hard ceiling sits above all of it. Even with every matched box made perfect, cross-domain AP reaches only 14.10, because just 22.9% of target objects get any prediction within 2.5 m, against 76.5% in-domain. Box quality cannot be repaired on objects that were never found.

03

The nine attempts

In the order they ran. Each was chosen by what the previous one ruled out, so the sequence is the argument.

01

Recalibrate INT8 on unlabeled target frames

Why. Post-training quantisation picks activation ranges from the source domain. If the target's activations fall outside them, everything saturates. This is the proposal's own cheapest intervention, and it needs no labels — only forward passes.

Calibrated on 540 target frames, scored on a disjoint 1,620. The split had to be built by hand: the converter had left the target's train file empty, so the config was about to calibrate on the very frames it scored.

Made it worse Car 1.83→0.15 Pedestrian 43.11 → 47.34
02

Lower the detection head's confidence floor

Why. The head discards proposals below 0.1. If target confidences are uniformly depressed, that floor alone could be erasing real detections.

It emitted two more boxes per frame and the score table did not move one digit. The cause turned out to be downstream: the evaluator's own converter hardcodes a detection_score > 0.45 cut before AP is ever computed, so the head's threshold never reaches the metric.

No effect Car 1.83→1.83
03

Lower the evaluator's operating point

Why. Attempt 02 exposed a fixed 0.45 cut — chosen on a domain where the median detection score is 0.987. On the target the median is 0.286, so that same cut discards 64% of predictions against 10% at the source. It looked like free recall.

Swept 0.45 → 0.01 on both domains. Car gained 0.47 and plateaued — but the source gained just as much (Pedestrian +1.10 source against +0.14 target). The hidden predictions are genuinely false positives.

Best result — but not adaptation Car 1.83→2.30
04

Neutralise the lidar intensity channel

Why. PointPillars consumes intensity as a feature. Two different lidars report it on different scales, which would put the pillar features off the training manifold.

The medians already agreed — 13 against 11 — and replacing intensity with a constant cost Pedestrian more than half its accuracy. The channel carries signal, not domain noise.

Made it much worse Car 1.83→0.23 Pedestrian 43.11 → 19.03
05

Blank the camera branch

Why. The target camera's optical axis sits about 16° lower than the source's. A fusion model fed geometry it never trained on might be actively suppressing detections its lidar branch would otherwise make. If so, gating the camera under shift is a free repair.

The opposite is true. With a constant image every class reads exactly zero and output drops to 2.1 boxes per frame. The camera is load-bearing.

Total collapse Car 1.83→0.00
06

Test whether sparser input explains it

Why. The target delivers 9,508 points inside the detection volume per frame against the source's 37,930 — four times sparser, after 54.5% of its cloud is cropped as structure above the ceiling. That is easily large enough to account for the loss.

The clean test is the reverse: thin the source to the target's density and score in-domain. Car lost 5.12 AP — 7.5% of the cross-domain gap. Small classes suffered far more, which is the opposite of the pattern we see across domains.

Density exonerated In-domain Car 69.70→64.58
07

Audit the camera calibration

Why. Attempt 05 proved the camera is essential. If the converter had broken its projection geometry, camera evidence would land in the wrong places and poison the fused features everywhere — which would look exactly like this failure.

Projecting ground-truth centres through the stored calibration: 100% land in front of the camera in both domains, 96.5% on the image for the source and 84.2% for the target. Sound. It did surface a real gap nobody had quantified — the target lens is 1.69× wider (focal 1293 against 2183), with objects at 31.6 m median depth against 68.3 m.

Calibration is sound Diagnostic, not a repair
08

Sweep the ground plane

Why. Target labels sit exactly where source labels do, but the target's point-cloud ground plane is about a metre lower, and predicted boxes land 0.49 m low. If points and labels disagreed in z, part of the measured "domain gap" would be our own converter — a bug, not a finding.

Seven shifts from −0.5 to +1.5 m, with z = 0 as control. No peak anywhere near +0.49. Car and Pedestrian decline monotonically as the cloud rises; the curve tracks how many points survive the crop, not label alignment.

Converter cleared Best 2.08 against a 1.73 control, on 400 frames
09

Confirm the one apparent win

Why. The sweep's −0.5 m point showed Car at 2.08 against a 1.73 control. Before claiming it, re-run on the full evaluation split — attempt 03 had already shown how readily an apparent repair turns out to be generic tuning.

It reversed sign. On 1,620 frames Car goes down 0.51 where 400 frames had promised up 0.35. The peak was subset noise at absolute values near 1–2 AP.

Did not replicate Car 1.83→1.32

05

Where it landed

Best Car AP after nine attempts: 2.30, from a baseline of 1.83, against 69.70 in-domain.

And the one positive is not adaptation. Lowering the evaluator's operating point helps the source domain as much as the target, which makes it threshold tuning that was available all along and has nothing to do with domain shift. The net domain-specific improvement from nine label-free interventions is zero.

The diagnosis explains why, and predicts it. The failure is conjunctive: four axes degrade moderately at once, and no intervention that leaves the weights untouched moves more than one of them. Nothing in the input space crosses an IoU threshold that needs three fixed simultaneously.

Two things survive as measured domain differences that no input patch can reach — a 1.69× focal-length gap feeding a camera branch the model cannot run without, and a recall collapse from 76.5% to 22.9% that caps every box-quality repair at 14.10 regardless of how good the boxes get.

The negative results are the result. Each attempt was designed to be falsifiable and several were designed specifically to catch our own pipeline rather than the domains. The ground-plane sweep was built to expose a converter bug and instead cleared it; the density test ran on the source so the control could not be confounded; the operating-point sweep ran on both domains, which is what exposed its own gain as generic. The cross-dataset measurement stands.

06

What would actually raise it

The evidence points one direction, and it is not another post-processing trick.

  1. Recall before box quality Perfect boxes on everything currently detected reach only 14.10, because 77% of objects are never found. Until detection improves, every box-refinement method is capped at a fifth of source performance. This orders the whole programme.
  2. Fine-tune the regression heads on pseudo-labels, backbone frozen The cheapest intervention that touches weights, and the only kind that can move heading, size and elevation together — which the conjunctive diagnosis says is required. Needs a GPU but not a full retrain, and no target labels.
  3. Source-side augmentation: random object scaling and local-structure features Both attack the axes we measured as broken — size at 0.786× height and 0.904× width, and an over-reliance on absolute geometry. Both are fully label-free, applied during source pre-training. Published gains are +27.19 AP and >21 mAP on vehicle-mounted transfers; whether either survives the move to a static roadside sensor is the open question, and a publishable one.
  4. Train a supervised oracle Without it, results can only be reported as absolute recovery. With it, every repair becomes a fraction of the recoverable gap — the convention the adaptation literature uses, and the difference between a result and an anecdote.
  5. Report at matched IoU, and report per class Car at 0.5 against Pedestrian and Cyclist at 0.25 is doing enormous work in every table on this page. Publishing both thresholds side by side turns a confusing class pattern into a stated property of the benchmark.
  6. Add a third domain One source and one target cannot separate sensor differences from geography. A second target pair is what turns a case study into an attribution.

The honest summary for the year ahead: this gap does not close from the input side. Nine attempts, each with a control, is the evidence for that rather than an assumption — and it is the case for the compute the next stage needs.
