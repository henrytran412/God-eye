Feasibility study · Davidson Student Scholars AY2026–27

Orientation is what breaks

A roadside 3D detector moved from Beijing to Munich keeps finding vehicles and keeps placing them correctly. It stops knowing which way they point — and that alone destroys its score.

The case, in five steps

One week, one borrowed board. Each step below is a measurement, and each links to the section that shows the working.

  1. The instrument is calibrated

    The pipeline reproduces NVIDIA's published DAIR-V2X-I accuracy to 0.02–0.04 AP on Car and Pedestrian. Nothing downstream is worth reading without this, and it is the part most feasibility studies skip.

    See §01 →

  2. The failure is real, and enormous

    The same frozen weights on a German intersection retain 0.2% of their Car accuracy at IoU 0.5 — 69.73 → 0.13 AP. This is not a marginal effect that needs teasing out of noise.

    See §04 →

  3. The cause is specific, and nobody has reported it

    Degradation is not uniform. Detection and position largely survive; the heading estimate collapses — 88% → 28% of vehicles within ±5°, with the median error unchanged at −0.2° in both domains, so it is not a coordinate bug. One head fails while the rest of the network holds.

    See §05 →

  4. It sits in unoccupied ground

    Efficiency research reports compressed accuracy in-domain. Domain-generalisation research reports cross-dataset accuracy at full precision, on datacentre GPUs. Neither reports compressed accuracy, cross-domain, on edge hardware — and the two literatures barely cite each other.

    See §07 →

  5. There is a cheap fix to test, not just a warning to issue

    Post-training quantisation takes its numeric ranges from a calibration set drawn from the source domain. Recalibrating on unlabelled target data needs forward passes only — no labels, no retraining, minutes of compute. If that closes the gap, the outcome is deployable rather than cautionary.

    See §07 →
And one step that did not land

The original hypothesis — that compression amplifies domain fragility — is not supported by this data. The per-class effects disagree in sign and sit at noise level. It is stated in full in §06 rather than buried, because the week's real output is a sharper question with a mechanism behind it, not a confirmed answer.

01

The pipeline reproduces published numbers

Before any new claim, the baseline had to prove itself. It does, to two decimal places.

NVIDIA publishes accuracy for V2XFusion on DAIR-V2X-I. Running their pretrained sparsity checkpoint through INT8 post-training quantisation on the Orin Nano, over the full 2,016-frame validation split, reproduces their table:

3D AP, easy / moderate / hard. Car and Pedestrian agree to within 0.04 AP.
ClassNVIDIA publishedThis runDifference
Car @0.582.06 / 69.70 / 69.75 82.08 / 69.70 / 69.75+0.02 / −0.00 / +0.00
Pedestrian @0.2551.13 / 48.86 / 49.22 51.17 / 48.87 / 49.22+0.04 / +0.01 / −0.00
Cyclist @0.2560.95 / 57.81 / 58.43 59.08 / 57.63 / 58.22−1.87 / −0.18 / −0.21

This matters more than it looks. Getting a 2022 research stack running on JetPack 7 took eight separate fixes — a C++14/C++17 mismatch against torch 2.6, a header removed from PyTorch in 1.11, a shared-memory limit that killed data loaders, and a hand-written CPU replacement for an IoU kernel because numba's CUDA backend segfaults on this board. After that much surgery, the fair question is whether anything silently broke. Landing on someone else's published values answers it.

Unresolved

Cyclist-easy sits 1.87 AP below published — roughly 30× the other deltas. The first hypothesis, calibration sampling, is ruled out: the FP16 run performs no calibration and shows the same gap. It is systematic, confined to the sparsest cell in the table, and recorded as open rather than explained away.

02

Compression is nearly free — at home

The in-domain cost of INT8, measured on this hardware rather than quoted.

3D AP, moderate difficulty, DAIR-V2X-I validation. Δ is FP16 minus INT8; positive means INT8 is worse.
ClassFP16INT8Δin
Car @0.569.7369.70+0.037
Pedestrian @0.2549.3948.87+0.518
Cyclist @0.2557.9057.63+0.263

Cars lose four hundredths of an AP point. Pedestrians, the smallest objects and the most quantisation-sensitive, lose six tenths. On its training distribution, INT8 is effectively free.

That is the claim this project puts a boundary condition on. Every deployment decision that cites a number like 0.037 is implicitly assuming it holds off-distribution, and nobody has checked.

03

Edge hardware cannot run it at full precision

Separately from accuracy: what the vendor's own deployment model costs on car-grade silicon.

NVIDIA's CUDA-BEVFusion, their pretrained weights, their TensorRT pipeline, real nuScenes data, running on the Orin Nano:

Both precisions miss the real-time line. 5.9 FPS and 8.4 FPS. Not just full precision — the quantised, vendor-optimised deployment model fails 10 Hz on this board.

And the stages that do not compress sum to 33.98 ms — 29% of the INT8 frame. A third of the budget is spent before quantisation gets a vote. The view transform is a scatter into a 200×200 BEV grid: memory-bound, so lowering precision does nothing for it.

Independent confirmation

An untrained FlashOcc-shaped network written from scratch for this study predicted 1.03× for the view transform. NVIDIA's production pipeline with trained weights measured 1.00×. Two unrelated implementations agree, so this is a property of the operator rather than of either codebase.

Against NVIDIA's published Orin AGX figures the gap is 3.04× at FP16 and 2.97× at INT8 — stable across precisions. The relative INT8 benefit matches AGX within 2% (1.42× vs 1.39×), so compression behaviour is hardware-portable even though absolute speed is not. Structured 2:4 sparsity is the exception: it returns 1.10–1.15× here against AGX's 1.17–1.24×, so sparsity gains extrapolated from a larger board would be overstated.

04

Moving countries costs almost everything

Zero-shot: the same frozen weights, evaluated on a German roadside intersection they have never seen.

Target domain: TUMTraf Intersection R2 — 2,160 synchronised frames from an elevated station near Munich, Basler camera plus Ouster OS1 LiDAR, across sunny day, sunny dusk and rainy night. Same task and same sensor modalities as DAIR-V2X-I, different country, intersection, sensor and vehicle fleet.

05

The failure is one head, not the network

This is the finding. The detector still sees; it stops knowing which way things face.

Matching every prediction to its nearest ground-truth vehicle and measuring the signed heading error separates a genuine failure from a coordinate bug — a frame or convention mismatch would appear as a constant offset.

DAIR-V2X-I — in-domain (n=411) TUMTraf — shifted (n=705)
Vehicle heading error against nearest-matched ground truth, FP16.
MeasureDAIR source TUMTraf target
Median signed error−0.2°−0.2°
Standard deviation12.4°24.0°
Within ±5°88%28%

The median is −0.2° in both domains. There is no constant offset, so this is not a conversion artefact. What changes is the spread: it doubles, and the error becomes multi-modal, clustering near +15…30° and −30…−60°.

Those clusters look like learned priors. The model appears to have absorbed the approach angles of the Beijing intersection it trained on, and the Munich intersection's roads meet at different angles.

This single mechanism explains the whole AP table. A 20° heading error on a 4.1 × 1.9 m box still overlaps its ground truth enough to clear IoU 0.25 but not IoU 0.5 — which is exactly why Car, scored at the stricter threshold, collapses to 0.13 while Pedestrian and Cyclist, scored at 0.25, retain 56% and 65%.

Falsifiable prediction

If the clusters are learned road geometry, they should align with TUMTraf's actual lane headings. That is a day's work to check, and it either confirms the mechanism or refutes it.

06

What this does not show

The original hypothesis was that compression amplifies domain fragility. This data does not support it.

Δ is FP16 minus INT8; positive means quantisation hurts. A clean amplification result would show the same sign in both columns and a ratio above 1.
ClassΔin DAIR Δout TUMTrafRatio
Car @0.5+0.037−0.654−17.9
Pedestrian @0.25+0.518−0.892−1.7

Negative result

The signs disagree. Two of three classes show INT8 performing better than FP16 out of domain, which is not physically meaningful. These differences are at noise level. Only Cyclist shows the predicted amplification.

One calibration seed, one target dataset and one model cannot separate a 1.3 AP effect from noise. Reporting this as a confirmed finding would be wrong.

On the mechanism the signal is cleaner, though still small. Quantisation adds no heading error in-domain and a little out of domain:

That is a hypothesis worth powering properly, not a result.

07

What the year would do

The week did not confirm the original question. It replaced it with a better one.

The question, restated

Not “how much accuracy is lost under domain shift?” — that is established, and this study re-measures it. Instead:

Which components of a 3D perception network are domain-fragile, and does compression make the fragile ones worse?

The heading result makes this concrete rather than rhetorical. Degradation is not uniform: detection and position transfer reasonably well, while orientation collapses. That is a property of a specific head, measurable per-component, and potentially fixable without retraining the whole network.

Why this is worth a year

  • It is unoccupied ground. Efficiency work reports compressed accuracy in-domain. Domain-generalisation work reports cross-dataset accuracy at full precision on datacentre GPUs. The two literatures barely cite each other, and nobody reports compressed accuracy cross-domain on edge hardware.
  • It has a mechanism, not just a number. Per-component fragility is a claim a reviewer can test and an engineer can act on. “Accuracy drops” is not.
  • There is a cheap candidate fix. PTQ derives its quantisation ranges from a calibration set drawn from the source domain. Calibrating on unlabelled target data needs forward passes only — no labels, no retraining, minutes of compute. If that recovers the gap, it is a deployable result rather than a warning.
  • It lands inside an existing research programme. Cross-dataset domain gaps in sensor layout, ego-frame conventions and class-name alignment are named gaps in the mentor's own documents. This study hit all three as concrete engineering problems and solved them.

What the first months would settle

  • Check the heading-cluster prediction against TUMTraf's lane geometry.
  • Repeat calibration across seeds with per-class confidence intervals, so the amplification question gets a real answer instead of a noisy one.
  • Add a second target domain, to separate “this pair of datasets” from “domain shift”.
  • Test target-domain calibration as the fix.
08

Limits, and what it took

Honest constraints on everything above:

  • Track A gives latency but never accuracy. NVIDIA ships a single example frame; real mAP needs the full nuScenes release.
  • Track B gives accuracy but never TensorRT latency. ONNX export fails on a PyTorch internal assertion in the TorchScript exporter under torch 2.6. That likely needs an x86 machine with an older torch.
  • One target dataset, one model, one calibration seed. Stated plainly in §06.
  • Class balance differs between domains. TUMTraf has no traffic cones, DAIR's largest class. Per-class AP is the honest unit; a single averaged mAP would hide this.
  • Two bugs were found and fixed during the cross-dataset work, both from assuming TUMTraf resembled DAIR: an image height of 1080 declared for 1920×1200 images, and a missing custom collate function. Pre-fix numbers are retained in the repository as a record.
Engineering byproduct — running this stack on JetPack 7

Getting a 2022 MMLab-based pipeline onto JetPack 7 / CUDA 12.6 / torch 2.6 / aarch64 required eight fixes, all committed:

  • -std=c++14 against torch 2.6 headers that need C++17 (76,835 errors from one flag)
  • THC/THC.h, removed from PyTorch in 1.11
  • Duplicate SparseConv2d registry entries
  • A 64 MB /dev/shm default that killed data loaders with SIGBUS
  • A CPU rotated-IoU implementation, because numba's CUDA backend segfaults on this board — a trivial kernel exits 139 while cuda.is_available() reports true
  • Four missing Python packages, and a pip --target trap that silently shadows the container's numpy

Two defects belong to the shared lab container image rather than this work: import scipy.sparse fails out of the box (scipy built against numpy 2 alongside numpy 1.26), and its PyTorch is compiled without torch.distributed.

The mmcv-full 1.7.2 wheel compiled against torch 2.6 on aarch64 has no public equivalent and is kept, along with a TUMTraf→DAIR converter whose camera choice, transform direction and ground alignment were each verified by measurement rather than assumption.
