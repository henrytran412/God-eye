# Unverified research claims (verification phase failed on usage limit)

### https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06682
  * ST3D++ is a fully label-free (unsupervised) target-domain adaptation method — it requires only unlabeled target point clouds plus the labeled source set, no target annotations and no target-domain retraining labels — and on Waymo→KITTI (2021, SECOND-IoU) it lifts car AP_3D from 27.48 (source-only) to 65.64, against a fully supervised oracle of 73.45, i.e. it recovers roughly 83% of the source-only-to-oracle gap; with the SN target size prior added it exceeds the oracle on all three categories.
  * The amount of the domain gap self-training recovers is strongly dependent on the dataset pair and object class, not uniform: gains are ~38–44 AP_3D on car for the two KITTI-target transfers but only ~10–11 (pedestrian) and ~9–10 (cyclist), and on Waymo→nuScenes car AP_3D moves only 17.24 (source-only) → 20.90 (ST3D++) against a 34.87 oracle — about 21% of the gap. A label-free self-training pipeline therefore cannot be assumed to close an arbitrary cross-dataset drop.
  * Statistical Normalization (SN) is not label-free — it consumes target-domain object-size statistics as extra supervision — and it can actively hurt when the shift is not primarily a size shift: in Table III SN drops cyclist AP_3D below source-only on nuScenes→KITTI (17.70/11.08 → 11.11/5.67 AP_BEV/AP_3D) and on Waymo→Lyft (33.47/28.90 → 30.21/23.44), and ST3D++(w/SN) also falls below plain ST3D++ on nuScenes→KITTI cyclist (30.87/23.93 → 13.57/12.64).

### https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Yang_ST3D_Self-Training_for_Unsupervised_Domain_Adaptation_on_3D_Object_Detection_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf
  * On Waymo→KITTI (car, IoU 0.7, KITTI moderate, AP over 40 recall positions), ST3D using only unlabeled target point clouds raises SECOND-IoU from 67.64/27.48 (AP_BEV/AP_3D, source-only) to 82.19/61.83, closing 92.97%/74.72% of the source-only-to-oracle gap; combined with SN it reaches 85.83/73.37, essentially matching the 83.29/73.45 oracle. PV-RCNN shows the same pattern (61.18/22.01 → 84.10/64.78, +82.45%/+70.71%; oracle 88.98/82.50).
  * Statistical Normalization (SN), which requires target-domain object-size statistics, can actively degrade BEV accuracy rather than help: on nuScenes→KITTI SN drops SECOND-IoU AP_BEV from 51.84 to 40.03 (closed gap −37.55%) and PV-RCNN from 68.15 to 60.48 (−36.82%), and on Waymo→Lyft SN drops PV-RCNN from 75.49/58.53 to 72.82/56.64 (−24.34%/−14.36%). ST3D needs no target size statistics and improves every evaluated pair.
  * Object-size domain shift is separable from the rest of the shift and is largely fixable on the source side alone: random object scaling (ROS), a source-domain augmentation that uses no target data whatsoever, lifts Waymo→KITTI SECOND-IoU from 67.64/27.48 to 78.07/54.67, i.e. ~+27.2 AP_3D, landing within 4.5 AP_3D of the weakly-supervised SN (78.96/59.20) that consumes target size statistics. ROS also compounds with self-training (ST3D w/o ROS 75.54/34.76 vs ST3D w/ ROS 82.19/61.83).

### https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08243
  * SF-UDA^3D performs LiDAR 3D detection domain adaptation with neither source data/annotations nor target-domain labels, and recovers 66% of the source-to-target oracle gap on nuScenes->KITTI and 30% on KITTI->nuScenes (3DV 2020, PointRCNN backbone, Car class).
  * Concrete cross-dataset numbers (Avg-AP): nuScenes->KITTI source-only 0.219 -> SF-UDA^3D 0.545 with target-trained oracle at 0.801; KITTI->nuScenes source-only 0.202 -> SF-UDA^3D 0.268 with oracle 0.422 — i.e. the untreated cross-dataset drop is roughly 3-4x, and adaptation closes only part of it.

### https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13891
  * DPO performs source-free test-time adaptation for LiDAR 3D detection: it adapts to unlabeled target point clouds in a single pass at test time, without target labels, without access to source training data, and without full retraining — exactly the supervision regime relevant to the research question.
  * On Waymo→KITTI (moderate, SECOND-IoU) DPO raises AP_3D from 27.48 (no adaptation) to 55.74 against a fully-supervised oracle of 73.45, recovering roughly 61% of the source-to-oracle AP_3D gap and ~91% of oracle AP_BEV; on nuScenes→KITTI it reaches 73.27/54.38 vs. 51.84/17.92 unadapted, closing 65.66% of the AP_3D gap.
  * Offline multi-epoch self-training (ST3D) still beats this single-pass TTA method on AP_3D for Waymo→KITTI (61.83 vs. 55.74), so the single-pass/online constraint costs accuracy relative to full self-training even though both are label-free — DPO only edges ST3D out on nuScenes→KITTI AP_3D (54.38 vs. 54.13).

### https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11355
  * On KITTI→nuScenes, Statistical Normalization (SN) — the size-shift correction baseline — achieves the BEST scale error of any method (ASE 0.168 vs 0.248 for direct transfer) while producing the WORST orientation error of any method (AOE 0.368 vs 0.212 for direct transfer, a 1.74x degradation), and also worsens translation error (ATE 0.227 vs 0.207). MLC-Net instead improves all three simultaneously (ATE 0.197 / ASE 0.179 / AOE 0.197). This is published quantitative evidence that a domain-adaptation method targeting object-size shift can actively degrade heading estimation as a separate, dissociable failure mode, and that AOE moves independently of ATE/ASE under domain shift.
  * MLC-Net requires no target-domain labels and no target-domain size statistics (source annotations only, single-frame input), yet outperforms SN/OT, which do require known target statistics. Reported PointRCNN numbers (2021): KITTI→Waymo 9.17 → 38.21 AP/L1 (SN 30.69); Waymo→KITTI 20.22 → 69.35 AP Easy (SN 61.93); KITTI→nuScenes 13.01 → 23.47 AP3D (SN 23.15); nuScenes→KITTI 49.13 → 71.26 AP Easy (SN 44.81, i.e. SN falls BELOW direct transfer on Easy for this pair).
  * The paper reports NO oracle / fully-supervised target-domain upper bound for any of its four transfer pairs, and does not compare against ST3D (concurrent, CVPR 2021); its only prior-art comparisons are SN and OT from Wang et al. [41], adversarial DA-Faster adapted to 3D, and (in supplementary) SF-UDA3D. Consequently the fraction of the oracle gap MLC-Net closes cannot be computed from this paper — only raw AP deltas over direct transfer are available.

### https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05988
  * Deploying a LiDAR 3D detector in an unfamiliar domain (different lidar, geography, or weather) causes a 70-90% drop in detection rate, manifesting as missed detections on densely observed objects, miscalibrated confidence, and more high-confidence false positives. Directly quantifies the magnitude of the cross-dataset drop this research question asks about, though for vehicle-mounted lidar-only transfer (Waymo/nuScenes/Lyft), not roadside/infrastructure sensors.
  * MS3D++ recovers most of the source-only-to-oracle gap in BEV using NO target-domain labels and NO source data — only unlabeled target sequences, several pre-trained multi-frame source detectors, and known sensor poses for ego-motion compensation. From the results tables: Waymo+Lyft -> nuScenes lifts Vehicle AP_BEV from 42.18 (Waymo source-only) to 50.34 against a 56.43 GT-trained ceiling (~76% of the gap); Lyft+nuScenes -> Waymo lifts Vehicle AP_BEV 58.34 -> 70.60 against 75.11 (~74%); Waymo+nuScenes -> Lyft lifts 72.09 -> 77.00 against 86.85 (~33%). AP_3D gaps close far less than AP_BEV, so the 'comparable to human labels' claim is BEV-specific. (2023 arXiv / IEEE T-IV 2024.)
  * A large share of apparent self-training gains is attributable to temporal point accumulation rather than domain adaptation: a multi-frame pre-trained source detector, with no adaptation at all, beats ST3D on Waymo -> nuScenes. Table X reports ST3D at Vehicle AP_BEV 35.90 (1-frame) / 38.80 (10-frame) vs MS3D++ 50.34 on the same transfer. This is a direct warning that source-free adaptation baselines must be compared under matched input framing, and that multi-frame accumulation is a cheap confounder.
  * The paper isolates heading/yaw error as a distinct failure mode from localization, and shows it is class-dependent: for elongated vehicles a heading error alone destroys IoU and produces poorly localized boxes, while pedestrians' BEV rotational symmetry means high IoU survives an outright wrong heading. This is evidence that standard IoU-based AP masks orientation failure for symmetric classes and conflates it with localization for elongated ones — an argument for reporting a heading-specific metric separately. The paper does not, however, propose an explicit heading-correction step or report a yaw-error metric.

### https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16237
  * MonoDLE (CVPR 2021) introduces a sub-task error-decomposition protocol for 3D detection that isolates orientation as a separately measurable failure axis: each 3D branch output (projected 3D center, depth, 3D size, heading angle) is swapped for ground truth one at a time, and also swapped in reverse (all-GT except one), with AP40 moderate on KITTI val re-measured each time. This is a directly reusable methodology for attributing a cross-dataset drop to yaw vs. localization vs. size, though the paper itself applies it only in-domain and to a monocular camera-only detector (no LiDAR, no domain shift).
  * Under in-domain KITTI conditions, orientation is a negligible bottleneck for monocular 3D detection relative to localization: replacing predicted orientation with ground truth raises moderate AP40 only from 11.12 to 11.88 (+0.76), and replacing predicted 3D size raises it only to 11.96, whereas replacing the full predicted 3D location with ground truth raises it to 78.84. This establishes the in-domain reference point against which any claim that heading error dominates under domain shift would have to stand out.

### https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03114
  * The paper isolates orientation error as its own failure mode by defining two separate angular metrics — half-range orientation error HOE = |(θ−θ̂) mod 180°| and full-range FOE = |(θ−θ̂) mod 360°| — reported alongside but independently of AP (localization/classification) and ℓ2 trajectory error. On nuScenes (Table II, 3 runs, IoU 0.7) the gap between them is enormous: the best half-range method Sin-cos-2x scores HOE 1.72°±0.04 but FOE 59.9°±1.4, proving that essentially all of its orientation error is 180° flip error rather than fine-grained angular regression error. This is a published precedent for reporting yaw error as a distinct, decomposed metric.
  * Detectors substantially infer vehicle heading from observed motion/trajectory rather than from object appearance, so heading estimation collapses for stationary vehicles. On nuScenes, Sin-cos-2x FOE is 4.7°±0.5 on moving actors but 59.9°±1.4 over all actors, and L1-sin is 5.0°±0.1 moving vs 60.2°±0.4 all — even after using predicted trajectory direction to lift half-range output to full range. Qualitatively the paper attributes parked-vehicle heading failures to the absence of a motion cue, which is direct evidence that heading rests on a non-appearance prior.
  * The front-vs-back of a vehicle is claimed to be not reliably distinguishable from the LiDAR point cloud itself, establishing an appearance-level ambiguity that makes 180° heading flips the dominant orientation failure mode for LiDAR-based BEV detectors — i.e. heading is under-determined by the sensor evidence and must be supplied by some other cue.

### https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08639
  * Prior domain-generalization methods for LiDAR 3D detection feed only global Cartesian point coordinates as input features, and this paper attributes their cross-domain failure to a shortcut: the detector latches onto absolute object position instead of object shape. This is a shortcut-learning/spurious-feature demonstration made specifically inside 3D detection (not image classification), and it is falsifiable by ablating input features.
  * Replacing global coordinates with an explicit local-structure encoding (GBlobs) yields large single-source cross-dataset gains over the prior state of the art: >21 mAP on Waymo->KITTI, 13 mAP on KITTI->Waymo, and 12 mAP on nuScenes->KITTI; multi-source DG gains are 17/12/5 mAP on Waymo/KITTI/ONCE. Paper is CVPR 2025; dataset pairs are autonomous-vehicle (vehicle-mounted) LiDAR, not roadside.
